Introduction

More than a permanent-wave machine

Gallia was associated with electric permanent waving, but its surviving catalogues document a considerably broader business in professional hairdressing equipment.

Catalogue No. 22 includes permanent-wave apparatus, drying installations, washing and antiseptic equipment, chairs, salon furnishings, preparations and accessories. It also refers to the Gallia-Journal, suggesting that technical products and professional communication formed part of the same commercial world.

This website brings together catalogues, trade advertisements, contemporary publications and other historical evidence. Its purpose is to document what the sources show, distinguish established facts from open questions, and make the material accessible to an international audience.

Research principles

Original documents take priority. Undated catalogues remain undated until their publication dates can be established. Claims about inventions, company relationships and distribution are assessed against contemporary evidence.

Selected evidence

A developing chronology

1920 · Contemporary trade source

A contemporary register based on 1920 periodical material identifies G. and F. Boudou in connection with a French hairdressing company. The precise corporate relationship requires further examination.

1929 · Budapest

An advertisement in the April issue of Fodrász Újság announces Gallia demonstrations and lectures in Budapest. The notice documents the planned events, not independently their completion.

Undated · Catalogue No. 22

The illustrated Catalogue général et liste des prix n° 22 documents permanent-wave equipment and a wide salon-product range. Its publication date remains unverified.

Undated · “La Marque de la Qualité”

A second digitised Gallia catalogue bears the company name, its Paris address and the brand slogan. The date is still being checked.

Open research questions

When were the different Gallia systems introduced? How did the company's structure and brand ownership evolve? What can contemporary records establish about international distributors?

Possible connections involving Gallia, Kadus and Charles Nessler are being investigated separately. No formal relationship is asserted here without documentary support.

About this project

Independent archival research

Gallia is part of the International Hairdressing History Project, an independent initiative documenting the people, equipment and techniques that shaped modern hairdressing.

The project is historical and non-commercial. Original catalogue scans and photographs remain with their respective rights holders; external links are provided for research and reference.
