Gallia · Company history

History of Gallia

From French permanent-wave equipment to a broader professional salon brand: a history reconstructed from catalogues, trade press and surviving archival evidence.

A French company in a changing profession

The early twentieth century transformed professional hairdressing. Electrically heated permanent-wave apparatus, specialised preparations and increasingly elaborate salon installations became part of a growing technical market. Gallia was one of the French names associated with this transformation.

Surviving documents connect the Gallia name with Félix Boudou & Cie in Paris and identify Félix and Gaston Boudou in the company's historical context. A contemporary trade source from 1920 refers to G. and F. Boudou in connection with a French technical and commercial hairdressing enterprise. The precise sequence of company formations and changes of legal identity still requires verification.

The evidence currently available does not justify assigning a firm founding year to Société Gallia or attributing every technical invention to a single individual.

Beyond the permanent-wave apparatus

The undated Gallia. Catalogue général et liste des prix n° 22 documents a notably broad product range. Alongside permanent-wave apparatus, including systems described as Gallia-Boncelle and Gallia Métro, the catalogue presents hair dryers and central drying installations, washing and antiseptic equipment, professional chairs, furniture, preparations and salon accessories.

Its supplementary price sheets and promotional letter show how the company presented products and prices to professional customers. The catalogue also mentions the Gallia-Journal, indicating a printed communication channel associated with the brand. Because the catalogue's publication date is not independently established, these products cannot yet be assigned precise introduction years on its evidence alone.

Training, demonstrations and international promotion

An advertisement in the Hungarian professional periodical Fodrász Újság (April 1929) announced lectures and demonstrations of Gallia permanent-wave machines in Budapest during the first half of that month. It referred to a teacher from the Gallia school, the director of its export department and a local contact at Rákóczi út 14.

This is direct evidence of planned international promotion and professional instruction. It is not, by itself, proof that the announced events took place or that a permanent Hungarian subsidiary existed.

The Gallia name and its corporate identity

Historical records use several forms of identification, including Société Gallia and Félix Boudou & Cie. The catalogue associated with the slogan La Marque de la Qualité gives the Paris address 8, rue Notre-Dame-de-Lorette. The surviving cover establishes the address and branding, but not an independently verified publication date.

Changes in legal ownership, the relationship between the Boudou names and the Gallia brand, and later commercial developments remain subjects for further documentary research. A brand name on a catalogue should not automatically be treated as proof of a particular legal company structure.

Selected documentary chronology

Research note: The archive identifier GAL-1936-001 is not sufficient evidence that the associated catalogue was published in 1936. Dates and technical firsts will be added only after the underlying documents have been checked.

Questions still under investigation

Further work will examine the Boudou family's respective roles, the introduction dates and technical distinctions of Gallia apparatus, the history of its training activities, and the distribution networks through which the company reached foreign markets.

Possible connections between Gallia, Kadus and Charles Nessler are being investigated separately. At present, this page does not assert a formal business relationship between them.
