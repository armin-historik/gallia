Technical context

The permanent wave as a salon system

Early electrically heated permanent waving involved more than a single machine. It depended on the interaction of hair preparation, winding, heating, timing and skilled professional handling.

Gallia's surviving catalogue evidence places permanent-wave apparatus alongside drying equipment, washing and antiseptic facilities, salon furniture, preparations and accessories. This breadth is important: the catalogue presents a professional equipment range, not merely one named invention.

The exact operating principles of individual Gallia models must be reconstructed from their instructions, drawings, patents and contemporary descriptions. The presence of a model name in a catalogue does not by itself establish its mechanism or date of introduction.

What the evidence allows

Documented: Gallia-Boncelle and Gallia Métro are identified in the project's record of General Catalogue No. 22.

Still to verify: their precise electrical arrangements, heating elements, winding methods, specifications and launch dates.

Historical practice

How heated permanent waving worked

This outline describes the general historical process of electrically heated permanent waving. It is not presented as a verified operating manual for either Gallia-Boncelle or Gallia Métro.

01 · Preparation

Hair and winding

Hair was prepared and arranged around suitable winding elements. Methods varied between manufacturers, periods and professional techniques.

02 · Controlled heating

Thermal treatment

Heating devices supplied energy to the wound hair. Safe application required attention to insulation, timing and the risk of excessive heat.

03 · Finishing

Cooling and styling

The treatment was completed according to the procedure in use, followed by removal of the winding elements and styling.

Historical explanation only; not instructions for performing a modern chemical or heated hair treatment.

Evidence and interpretation

What we still need to establish

Technical history depends on distinguishing what an advertisement promises from what a machine demonstrably does. The next stage is a systematic comparison of catalogue illustrations, price lists, patent drawings and contemporary trade accounts.

In particular, we need to check whether Gallia-Boncelle and Gallia Métro were complete apparatus, named configurations or variants within a larger product family. No conclusion is drawn here before the catalogue pages have been verified.

Catalogue research checklist

For each machine: exact printed name; catalogue page; image or drawing; dimensions; voltage and power (if stated); heating method; winding accessories; price; instructions; and the date of the document.

Only details visible in a source should be entered as established facts.
