Félix & Gaston Boudou

Two names in the historical record of Gallia

An examination of what contemporary trade material and surviving Gallia catalogues can establish about the individuals associated with this French hairdressing-equipment enterprise.

Biographical research

People, roles and evidence

Félix Boudou and Gaston Boudou are associated with the Gallia name in historical records. Their precise responsibilities, family relationship and individual contributions require careful documentary reconstruction.

Gallia’s catalogues and advertisements tell us much about the business and its products. They do not, by themselves, provide complete biographies. This page therefore separates directly documented associations from questions that remain open.

Evidence standard

Names on a company imprint do not automatically identify an inventor, founder or sole owner. Likewise, a shared surname does not establish a particular family relationship.

Research status: Biographical details are provisional until supported by civil, company, patent or contemporary professional records.

What the documents show

Félix Boudou

The imprint Félix Boudou & Cie appears in the Gallia source descriptions and in a contemporary Hungarian trade advertisement published in April 1929. That advertisement identifies the Paris address as 8, rue Notre-Dame-de-Lorette.

Documented association · 1929

The advertisement in Fodrász Újság connects the Gallia brand with the firm name Félix Boudou et Cie and a Paris business address. It also promotes demonstrations in Budapest.

This is evidence of a business name and marketing activity, not proof of Félix Boudou’s personal attendance in Hungary.

Gallia’s illustrated general catalogue No. 22 is another important source for the company’s commercial activity. Its accompanying archive description identifies Société Gallia / Félix Boudou & Cie, but the catalogue’s publication date remains unverified.

Professional and technical context

Gaston Boudou

Gaston Boudou appears in the project’s register of material from the 1920 French hairdressing press as G. Boudou, alongside F. Boudou, in connection with a French technical and commercial hairdressing organisation. This is a useful research lead, but the underlying periodical pages must be checked before assigning precise titles or dates.

Archival research lead · 1920

The project’s consolidated 1920 register identifies G. and F. Boudou in association with the Société Française Technique et Commerciale de la Coiffure. The original periodical passage and its corporate terminology require verification.

An internal research register is a finding aid; it is not a substitute for the underlying primary document.

Later historical accounts associate Gaston Boudou with the development and promotion of permanent-wave technology. For this website, specific invention claims, patent attributions and claims about collaboration will be added only after examination of the relevant primary sources.

The wider enterprise

The Boudou names and the Gallia business

Gallia should not be understood solely through the biographies of two individuals. The surviving catalogue No. 22 presents a broad professional range: permanent-wave apparatus, hair-drying installations, washing and antiseptic equipment, salon furniture, accessories and preparations.

The same catalogue description mentions the Gallia-Journal, suggesting an interest in professional communication as well as equipment sales. An April 1929 advertisement announced lectures and demonstrations in Budapest, including the participation of a Gallia-school instructor and an export-department representative.

These sources support a picture of a business combining products, instruction and overseas promotion. They do not yet establish who personally directed each activity.
